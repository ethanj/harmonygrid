In the end, that’s what I did, too. In 
fact, what I wound up doing with this 
program had nothing to do with har¬ 
mony. Instead, I set up a percussion grid 
and loaded up my sampler with a variety 
of percussion sounds assigned to differ¬ 
ent keys. I then devised scales of percus¬ 
sion timbres and chords that would trig¬ 
ger different combinations of instru¬ 
ments. This produced some interesting 
results, especially if the metronome was 
set to absurdly high speeds. One other 
nice touch is that Version 1.5 added 
MIDI velocity control keys, which pro¬ 
vide three user-settable accent levels to 
the various real-time controls. 
SO WHAT'S IT GOOD FOR? 
I feel Harmony Grid is good for explor¬ 
ing fuzzy, informal questions on the 
order of, “What does the X scale sound 
like?” in a nonrigorous way. It’s hard to 
answer such questions verbally (try it!), 
yet with a tool like Harmony Grid, the 
unsophisticated user gets to try it out 
and have fun in the process. Its novel 
graphics approach will probably appeal 
more to untrained users than to those 
accustomed to traditional notation. 
Keep an eye on Hip Software; the 
people there don’t think like other 
people in the business, and considering 
that Harmony Grid is their first commer¬ 
cial effort, I’m looking forward to what 
they come up with in the future.
