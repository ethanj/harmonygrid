DOCUMENTATION 
The main body of the manual consists of 
three short introductory/tutorial chap¬ 
ters, a reference manual, and a pot¬ 
pourri of various subjects entitled “See¬ 
ing Harmony Theory.” The manual con¬ 
cludes with brief performing tips and 
summary material; there are also some 
reference cards concerning scales and 
performance options. 
The manual seems to be the weakest 
part of the product. I have no problems 
with the reference section, but the open¬ 
ing tutorial—presumably aimed at users 
who are newcomers to both the pro¬ 
gram and to music theory in general— 
contains a great deal of undefined music 
vocabulary. A random scan of a couple 
of pages shows terms such as minor to¬ 
nality, root, unstable pitch, harmonic 
suspense, and many others. 
Some of this is cleared up in the chap¬ 
ter “Seeing Harmony Theory,” but that 
comes about twenty pages too late. This 
chapter attempts to cover a huge 
amount of ground (everything from a 
history of pitch systems to Harmony 
Grid-specific topics) in 41 small pages, 
and the assumed reader’s level of sophis¬ 
tication fluctuates wildly—from as basic 
as not knowing the difference between 
major and minor to as complex as fol¬ 
lowing the derivation of 12-tone equal 
temperament.

look at this manual and decide for
